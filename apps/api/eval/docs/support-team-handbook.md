# Customer Support Team Handbook

This handbook describes how the customer support team handles tickets and incidents. It complements the engineering on-call runbook, which covers technical response.

## Ticket priorities

Tickets are classified P1 to P3. P1 means the customer cannot use the product at all. P2 means an important feature is broken. P3 covers questions and minor issues. The support agent who first reads the ticket sets the priority and may raise it at any time but should lower it only after talking to the customer.

## Response targets

Support agents must send a first response to a P1 ticket within 15 minutes, to a P2 ticket within 4 hours and to a P3 ticket within 1 business day. Targets are measured by the helpdesk and reviewed weekly. Auto-replies do not count as a first response.

## Escalating to engineering

Escalate to the on-call engineer through the paging tool when a P1 ticket cannot be resolved with the troubleshooting guides. Include the customer name, the time the problem started and any error messages. Do not promise the customer a fix time until engineering has confirmed one.

## Status page and customer updates

During an incident, support posts customer-facing updates in the ticket queue and links the public status page. Updates should be short, factual and free of internal jargon. If the incident lasts longer than two hours, agents contact each affected enterprise customer directly.

## After an incident

Within 2 business days of a major incident, support sends affected customers a short summary that explains what happened and what has been done to prevent it. The summary is reviewed by the support lead and is separate from the detailed engineering postmortem.

## Shift handover

Support is staffed in three regional shifts. The outgoing agent writes a handover note every day at 09:00 UTC listing open escalations and customers waiting for a reply. New agents shadow a senior colleague for their first two weeks before taking tickets alone.

## Tone and wording

Be warm, direct and specific. Thank customers for their patience once, not in every message. Never blame the customer, and never speculate about the root cause before engineering has confirmed it.
